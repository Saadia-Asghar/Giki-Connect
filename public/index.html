    How to use GIKI-Connect

    Use the three tabs at the top like chapters: read this guide first, try the student story second, and use the admin area only if you are pretending to post a campus event.

    Tap any heading below to open or close that part of the guide.

    How to move around

    How to use (this tab) — stays open while you read; you can come back anytime.

    Student — scroll from the short intro down through the numbered steps. Answer the sliders and checkboxes in order, then press Find my interest tribe. Your result appears lower on the same page: a tribe label, a row of tribe cards (yours is highlighted), suggested events, and gentle “what to try next” ideas. Use the small “On this page” links if you want to jump between intro, form, and results.

    GIKI admin — switch here to pretend you are campus staff: enter the admin phrase already filled in for the demo, describe an event (time, place, what it is), tick hobby tags, and optionally tick which interest tribes should see it first. Publish, then go back to Student and run the form again to see whether your event shows up in the suggestions list.

    What your tribe result depends on

    When you press Find my interest tribe, the demo places you in one of eight interest tribes that were learned ahead of time from the course survey. That placement is driven only by what you enter on the form in this order of importance:

    • The hobbies you tick.
    • Society hours per week (treated as zero if you say you are not a society member).
    • How comfortable you say you are meeting new people.
    • The social sliders — roughly, how tight your close-friend circle is across province and faculty, together with how many close friends you say you have.

    What does not change the tribe number in this demo: the faculty and year you pick, and the named societies you tick. Those answers travel with your submission so a reader can see the full story, but they are not part of the eight-tribe grouping unless the project is rebuilt with new analysis.

    What you can get to know

    Big picture. The project talks about living on a residential campus but still forming small circles by faculty, home region, or batch; how societies and shared hobbies can act as a bridge; and how “interest tribes” describe taste and habits rather than copying demographics.

    From the numbers at the top of Student. You see how many anonymised profiles sit in the survey pool, that there are eight tribes, and that the match is grounded in coursework data—not a random picker.

    After you run the form. You see which tribe you landed in, a plain-language line about how tight your social picture looks, event ideas matched to that tribe and your hobbies, and anonymised “say hi” style prompts. The expandable notes under the result explain, in words, how the tribe was chosen without asking you to trust a black box.

    Behind the scenes (one sentence). The survey analysis and tribe labels were finished once for the course; this site applies that same grouping and adds readable suggestions on top — it does not relearn from your clicks while you explore.

    If the page or button does not work

    The demo needs the small program that ships with the project to be running on your computer. Start it the way your instructor or README describes (for example the START_APP shortcut or running the main server script). A message in the same window will show a local web address — open that exact address in your browser and use the site from there.

    If you opened the web page from a preview inside an editor, or by double-clicking a file in a folder, the button may not respond. Close that tab, start the program, and open only the address the program tells you. If an old tab is stuck, try a full refresh or open the address again in a new tab.
